# Farlight

Farlight is a live window on Earth from two distances at once.
From L1, NOAA’s DSCOVR spacecraft points NASA’s EPIC camera back at the sunlit face of the planet and takes a full-disk photo every hour or so — the modern Blue Marble. Farlight pulls those images and lets you scrub through a day: watch continents rotate into view, read the centroid (which part of Earth is facing the camera), and stand a million miles “outside.”
From low Earth orbit, the International Space Station crosses that same disk every ~90 minutes. Farlight plots the live ground track, names who is aboard, and — once you share your location — tells you the next time the station will pass over you.
Two vantage points, one planet:

Outside: EPIC natural/enhanced full-disk Earth from DSCOVR
Overhead: live ISS position, crew board, next pass over your city

Data comes from public NASA / NOAA / community APIs. No account required to look.
